A neuropeptide atlas of the mouse ventromedial hypothalamus

Which neuropeptides neurons of the ventromedial hypothalamic nucleus (VMH) make, which neuropeptide receptors they carry, where in the nucleus each is found, and where the matching ligands could come from.

Built from the Allen Brain Cell Atlas, release 20250531, and the unified anatomical atlas of Chon et al. 2019. Every number in this documentation is generated from the pipeline's tables when the site is built.

What this is

The atlas answers three questions for {{n_lig}} peptide precursor genes and {{n_rec}} receptor genes, curated by hand:

  • Peptides made. Which precursor genes VMH neurons express, and in which part of the nucleus.
  • Receptors carried. Which neuropeptide receptor genes VMH neurons express, and where.
  • Ligand sources (supplemental). For each receptor found in VMH, whether a matching peptide is made inside VMH, in a region that projects to it, or outside the brain.
All of this is transcript evidence. It shows which peptides and receptors VMH neurons can make. It does not show that a peptide is released, or that a receptor protein sits on the cell body.

Reading the explorer

The explorer (Map, All genes, Ligand sources and Notes, at the top of this page) has a gene list on the left, a view in the middle and a card for the selected gene on the right.

Views

  • Map. Neurons around the VMH in one tissue section of one brain, with the atlas compartments drawn as outlines. Neurons can be coloured by the selected gene, by compartment, or by VMH supertype. Unregistered neurons have an unknown compartment. Pointing at a neuron (or tapping it) rings it and shows its cluster, its compartment after alignment, and its values for the selected gene. Where dots overlap, the note describes the one drawn on top. The Download buttons in the corner of the map save the map in view, with a title, its legend and its caption, as a PNG image or as a vector SVG or PDF file whose shapes and text stay editable. The saved figure is always drawn on white, whatever the colours of the page. The map keeps its size when the colouring or the estimate is switched.
  • All genes. Genes meeting the screen by compartment or supertype. Compare projected brain mixes, or select a brain to compare its measured and projected fractions.
  • Ligand sources. For each receptor in VMH, where a matching peptide is made.
  • Notes. Your own marks and remarks. The star beside the gene name in the card marks a gene as important, and the box under it holds a note on that gene; marked and annotated genes carry a sign in the gene list, which can be narrowed to them. The Notes view lists them with a box for general remarks, and exports everything as Markdown (to read), CSV (for a spreadsheet) or JSON (which Import reads back, merging into what is there). Marks and notes are kept in the browser you are using and are sent nowhere, so export them to keep or share them.

Measured in situ and Projected

When the map is coloured by a gene, the Estimate switch chooses between two things that are easy to confuse:

  • Measured in situ shows each neuron's own signal for that gene in that brain. It exists only for genes on the brain's panel: {{panel_sentence}} Genes measured in the selected brain carry a dot in the gene list. For any other gene the button is disabled, and the toolbar offers the brains where the gene was measured. Color intensity uses a gene-specific scale for the brain. The background test uses the original signal before display rounding.
  • Projected exists for every gene. Each neuron shows the share of cells of its transcriptomic cluster that express the gene in the single-cell sequencing data. It is a property of the neuron's cluster, not a measurement of that neuron.

Numbers

  • Compartment values are percent of neurons in that compartment with transcript detection by the indicated method.
  • Supertype values are percent of cells of that supertype expressing above background. The labels broad, subset and sparse mean at least 50%, 20 to 50%, and 10 to 20%; background means the gene is not called in that type.
  • The gene list is ordered by the projected percent of core neurons in the male Allen brain.

Data

MERFISH is multiplexed error-robust fluorescence in situ hybridization. The cell types are those of the Allen whole-mouse-brain taxonomy: two VMH subclasses, named for Nr5a1 and Fezf1, divided into nine supertypes. These are transcriptomic lineages and do not respect compartment borders, so results are given both by compartment and by supertype.

Which cells count as VMH

A neuron belongs to the compartment whose atlas label covers its position, after two corrections. The atlas itself sits exactly in the Allen CCF: its plates are drawn on template images that are identical, pixel for pixel, to every tenth coronal plane of the Allen template (plate 1 is plane 94, counting from zero).

Interpolation between plates

The atlas plates were each drawn separately, and tissue sections are not parallel to them: within one section the VMH spans 50 to 220 µm along the anterior-posterior axis. Reading the nearest plate would stitch two or three drawings together in one section, with seams. The compartments are therefore interpolated between plates. Each plate's outlines become signed distance fields, smoothed in the plane with a 15 µm Gaussian, and joined across plates by a monotone cubic. Smoothing can shift boundary pixels at a published plate. Against a nearest-plate reading it changes the label of {{relabel_range}} of the neurons in or next to VMH, nearly all at compartment borders or the outer edge.

Alignment

Read at the released coordinates, the atlas VMH sits posterior of the VMH cell types in every brain. Each brain is therefore aligned by one rigid shift of its cells, chosen to maximise overlap between neurons of the two VMH subclasses and the atlas VMH as a whole. The same neurons determine the shift and its F1 score. Independent anatomical landmarks would be needed to validate the subdivision borders.

The two atlases compared

The Allen CCF annotation has a VMH without subdivisions; the unified atlas has the compartments. The comparison uses nearest-plate unified labels. Both atlases are drawn in the same CCF space, so they can be compared directly, and each can be given its own best shift so that neither is penalised for the registration offset of the cells. The Allen VMH and the unified core have the same volume but do not coincide: {{overlap_sentence}}

F1 is the harmonic mean of two shares: neurons inside the outline that are VMH types, and VMH-type neurons that fall inside the outline. Once each outline has its own best shift, the Allen CCF VMH has the highest F1 in every brain, and a larger share of the neurons inside it are VMH types than inside the unified core. The unified atlas is used here for its compartments, which the Allen annotation lacks, and not because its outer boundary is more accurate. One caution applies to the test itself: the anatomical names in the cell-type taxonomy were assigned partly from where cells fall in Allen CCF space, so agreement with the Allen annotation is not fully independent.

Calling expression

Abundant hypothalamic peptide transcripts leak into every droplet during single-cell sequencing, so a nonzero count is not evidence of expression. For each gene and library, the background is the lowest mean among non-neuronal cell classes. A cell type is called expressing when at least 10% of its cells exceed what that background predicts and its mean is at least 3 times the background. When the excess fraction reaches 50%, a 1.5-fold enrichment also qualifies. Sparse calls must also be at least twice the typical neuronal type. Two receptors, Insr and Igf1r, are detected in most neurons but no higher than in non-neuronal cells, so the test cannot place them.

Measured and projected

  • Projected. The compartment's mix of cell types, from the spatial data, times each type's ambient-adjusted detection fraction from the sequencing data. Types below the call threshold contribute zero. Droplet dropout can hide genuinely expressing cells. Available for all genes.
  • Measured. For genes on a spatial panel, the fraction of the compartment's neurons whose signal exceeds the 95th percentile of non-neuronal cells in the same brain.
  • What a measured value is worth. The Allen panel carries {{n_blank}} blank probes that target no transcript. Put through the same rule, a blank probe passes in a median of {{blank_median}} of a compartment's neurons, and the 95th percentile across blank probes is {{blank_floor}}, depending on the compartment; {{blank_reaching}} of them reach 10%. A measured fraction of 10% is therefore inside what arises with nothing to detect. A measured value counts on its own only when it exceeds that 95th percentile for its compartment. The Zhuang matrices carry no blank probes, so a measured value from those brains cannot be tested and never brings a gene into the lists by itself; it is still shown, and it still confirms a projection of at least 10%.

Pooling genes and compartments gives Spearman correlations of {{spearman_sentence}}. Ranking the five compartments within each gene gives median correlations of {{within_gene}}. Genes with a constant estimate have no defined rank correlation. Measured levels are platform-specific, so a difference between the Allen brain and the Zhuang brains is not a sex difference.

Main results

{{n_lig_hit}} of {{n_lig}} precursor genes and {{n_rec_hit}} of {{n_rec}} receptor genes pass the screen: at least 10% of the neurons of a dorsomedial, central, ventrolateral or shell compartment, in at least one of the three brains, by the projection or by a measured value that clears the blank-probe control (see Measured and projected). The evidence column summarizes the screen. Numeric columns show the Allen male projection. Gene cards identify each brain and compartment where the methods fall on opposite sides of 10%, and say whether a measured value counted.

The undivided anterior and posterior ends are shown in every table but do not decide whether a gene is listed. After alignment, {{ends_share}} of the neurons inside them are VMH cell types, against {{three_share}} in the dorsomedial, central and ventrolateral compartments, so in two brains a value from the ends mostly describes neighbouring nuclei.

Left out by these two rules: {{weak_genes}} reach 10% only by a measured value that the controls do not support, and {{ends_genes}} reach 10% only in the undivided ends. Their cards show the numbers.

Some calls sit on the threshold. Listed with a best observation less than one percentage point above its threshold: {{borderline_listed}}. Missing it by less than one point: {{borderline_unlisted}}. These can change sides with a small change in alignment: correcting the position of the atlas by 10 µm moved Gal onto the list and Cckar off it. Their cards are marked.

Where ligands come from

Local evidence is the screen of Main results. Ambiguous non-neuronal background alone gives an unresolved call. Afferent candidates require ligand-producing clusters in a region with a mouse tracing citation ({{n_tracing}} of {{n_regions}} curated regions). The final cluster classifier achieves {{calibration}} on the data used to choose its threshold. Five-fold validation holding out entire hypothalamic clusters gives F1 {{held_out_f1}}. Transfer accuracy in other regions is unknown.

Endocrine sources are curated by peptide product, including intestinal GLP-1 from Gcg. Receptor complexes require evidence for same-cell partners. Conditional and proposed partners are listed separately on receptor cards.

Limits

  • Transcript, not protein. A precursor needs processing before it yields a peptide, one precursor can yield several peptides, and a receptor's protein may be trafficked to terminals outside VMH.
  • The blank-probe control is a 95th percentile. One blank probe in twenty exceeds it, so among the genes on the Allen panel a few would pass by chance. {{measured_alone}}, with no projection of 10% anywhere ({{measured_alone_elsewhere}}); treat that as a lead to confirm, not as a finding. Conversely, the control cannot rescue a genuine low-abundance receptor whose measured fraction sits inside the noise band, and the Zhuang brains offer no such control at all.
  • Sparse receptors are under-counted. Droplet sequencing misses low-abundance transcripts, so a detection fraction can underestimate prevalence. Values below 20% are the least secure.
  • Compartments stand in for molecular territories. The atlas ventrolateral compartment is narrower than the territory of the Esr1-rich supertypes. For questions about that population, read the supertype numbers.
  • Alignment is a rigid shift, and the atlas between plates is interpolated. The shift corrects the offset but not local distortion. Positions between plates follow an interpolation of the published drawings, not a published label.
  • The ligand-source table is not projection-specific, and the strength and species of the supporting evidence vary. Distribution-only and uncited entries remain provisional.
  • Unchecked inputs. The receptor coupling shown in the explorer is from general pharmacology and has not been checked against a reference.
