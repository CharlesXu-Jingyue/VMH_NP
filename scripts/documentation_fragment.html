A neuropeptide atlas of the mouse ventromedial hypothalamus

Which neuropeptides neurons of the ventromedial hypothalamic nucleus (VMH) make, which neuropeptide receptors they carry, where in the nucleus each is found, and where the matching ligands could come from.

Built from the Allen Brain Cell Atlas, release 20250531, and the unified anatomical atlas of Chon et al. 2019. Every number in this documentation is generated from the pipeline's tables when the site is built.

What this is

The atlas answers three questions for {{n_lig}} peptide precursor genes and {{n_rec}} receptor genes, curated by hand:

  • Peptides made. Which precursor genes VMH neurons express, and in which part of the nucleus.
  • Receptors carried. Which neuropeptide receptor genes VMH neurons express, and where.
  • Ligand sources (supplemental). For each receptor found in VMH, whether a matching peptide is made inside VMH, in a region that projects to it, or outside the brain.
All of this is transcript evidence. It shows which peptides and receptors VMH neurons can make. It does not show that a peptide is released, or that a receptor protein sits on the cell body.

Reading the explorer

The explorer (Map, All genes and Ligand sources, at the top of this page) has a gene list on the left, a view in the middle and a card for the selected gene on the right.

Views

  • Map. Neurons around the VMH in one tissue section of one brain, with the atlas compartments drawn as outlines. Neurons can be coloured by the selected gene, by compartment, or by VMH supertype.
  • All genes. Every gene of the chosen class by compartment or by supertype, as a table.
  • Ligand sources. For each receptor in VMH, where a matching peptide is made.

Measured in situ and Projected

When the map is coloured by a gene, the Estimate switch chooses between two things that are easy to confuse:

  • Measured in situ shows each neuron's own signal for that gene in that brain. It exists only for genes on the brain's panel: {{panel_sentence}} Genes measured in the selected brain carry a dot in the gene list. For any other gene the button is disabled, and the toolbar offers the brains where the gene was measured.
  • Projected exists for every gene. Each neuron shows the share of cells of its transcriptomic cluster that express the gene in the single-cell sequencing data. It is a property of the neuron's cluster, not a measurement of that neuron.

Numbers

  • Compartment values are percent of neurons in that compartment that express the gene.
  • Supertype values are percent of cells of that supertype expressing above background. The labels broad, subset and sparse mean at least 50%, 20 to 50%, and 10 to 20%; background means the gene is not called in that type.
  • The gene list is ordered by the projected percent of core neurons in the male Allen brain.

MERFISH is multiplexed error-robust fluorescence in situ hybridization. The cell types are those of the Allen whole-mouse-brain taxonomy: two VMH subclasses, named for Nr5a1 and Fezf1, divided into nine supertypes. These are transcriptomic lineages and do not respect compartment borders, so results are given both by compartment and by supertype.

Interpolation between plates

The atlas plates were each drawn separately, and tissue sections are not parallel to them: within one section the VMH spans 50 to 220 µm along the anterior-posterior axis. Reading the nearest plate would stitch two or three drawings together in one section, with seams. The compartments are therefore interpolated between plates. Each plate's outlines become signed distance fields, smoothed in the plane with a 15 µm Gaussian, and joined across plates by a monotone cubic. The interpolated atlas reproduces every plate at its own position and never leaves the range of the two neighbouring plates. Against a nearest-plate reading it changes the label of {{relabel_range}} of the neurons in or next to VMH, nearly all at compartment borders or the outer edge.

Alignment

Read at the released coordinates, the atlas VMH sits posterior of the VMH cell types in every brain. Each brain is therefore aligned by one rigid shift of its cells, chosen to maximise overlap between neurons of the two VMH subclasses and the atlas VMH as a whole. Compartment identity plays no part in the fit.

"Core" pools the dorsomedial, central and ventrolateral compartments with the undivided ends of the nucleus. The shell is reported on its own, because only about half of its neurons are VMH types.

The two atlases compared

The Allen CCF annotation has a VMH without subdivisions; the unified atlas has the compartments. Both are drawn in the same CCF space, so they can be compared directly, and each can be given its own best shift so that neither is penalised for the registration offset of the cells. The Allen VMH and the unified core have the same volume but do not coincide: {{overlap_sentence}}

{{comparison_rows}}

F1 is the harmonic mean of two shares: neurons inside the outline that are VMH types, and VMH-type neurons that fall inside the outline. Once each outline has its own best shift, the Allen CCF VMH has the highest F1 in every brain, and a larger share of the neurons inside it are VMH types than inside the unified core. The unified atlas is used here for its compartments, which the Allen annotation lacks, and not because its outer boundary is more accurate. One caution applies to the test itself: the anatomical names in the cell-type taxonomy were assigned partly from where cells fall in Allen CCF space, so agreement with the Allen annotation is not fully independent.

Calling expression

Abundant hypothalamic peptide transcripts leak into every droplet during single-cell sequencing, so a nonzero count is not evidence of expression. For each gene and library, the background is the lowest mean among non-neuronal cell classes. A cell type is called expressing when at least 10% of its cells exceed what that background predicts and its mean is at least 3 times the background. Sparse calls must also be at least twice the typical neuronal type. Two receptors, Insr and Igf1r, are detected in most neurons but no higher than in non-neuronal cells, so the test cannot place them.

Measured and projected

  • Projected. The compartment's mix of cell types, from the spatial data, times each type's fraction of expressing cells, from the sequencing data. Available for all genes.
  • Measured. For genes on a spatial panel, the fraction of the compartment's neurons whose signal exceeds the 95th percentile of non-neuronal cells in the same brain.

Across genes and compartments the two agree with Spearman correlations of {{spearman_sentence}}. Measured levels are platform-specific, so a difference between the Allen brain and the Zhuang brains is not a sex difference.

Where ligands come from

Release cannot be read from transcript data. Three kinds of availability stand in for it. Local: VMH neurons make the peptide. Afferent: neurons in a region reported to project to VMH make it; the {{n_regions}} regions are a hand-curated list. Endocrine: the ligand is made mainly outside the brain.

Source of a matching peptideReceptorsWhich

"Afferent" asks whether a region that projects to VMH contains neurons that make the peptide. It does not ask whether those particular neurons are the ones that project.

Limits

  • Transcript, not protein. A precursor needs processing before it yields a peptide, one precursor can yield several peptides, and a receptor's protein may be trafficked to terminals outside VMH.
  • Sparse receptors are under-counted. Droplet sequencing misses low-abundance transcripts, so receptor percentages are lower bounds. Values below 20% are the least secure.
  • Compartments stand in for molecular territories. The atlas ventrolateral compartment is narrower than the territory of the Esr1-rich supertypes. For questions about that population, read the supertype numbers.
  • Alignment is a rigid shift, and the atlas between plates is interpolated. The shift corrects the offset but not local distortion. Positions between plates follow an interpolation of the published drawings, not a published label.
  • The ligand-source table is not projection-specific, and its list of afferent regions was curated by hand and has not been checked against tracing data.
  • Unchecked inputs. The receptor coupling shown in the explorer is from general pharmacology and has not been checked against a reference.
